Recommended time: 120 minutes.

This practice examination is designed to simulate the PSYC 210 midterm and deliberately mixes material from Weeks 1–6.

Section A — Multiple Choice

Question 1

Week 1

A researcher randomly assigns participants to a quiet or noisy study condition and measures recall. What design is this?

  • A. Experimental
  • B. Quasi-experimental
  • C. Correlational
  • D. Case study
TipAnswer

Correct answer: A. Experimental

Random assignment plus manipulation makes this a true experiment.

Question 2

Week 6

Using μ=80, SE=3.00, and x̄=86.00, what is Z_obt?

  • A. 2.00
  • B. 1.00
  • C. 0.50
  • D. 3.00
TipAnswer

Correct answer: A. 2.00

The observed mean is exactly two standard errors above the null mean.

Question 11

Week 6

For a nondirectional test of whether a treatment changes μ from 80, which hypotheses are correct?

  • A. H0: μ=80; H1: μ≠80
  • B. H0: μ≠80; H1: μ=80
  • C. H0: x̄=80; H1: x̄≠80

TipAnswer

Correct answer: A. H0: μ=80; H1: μ≠80

The hypotheses concern the population mean and a nondirectional alternative uses ≠.

Question 12

Section B — Integrated Short Answer

Integrated Problem 1

Weeks covered: 5

A population has μ=50 and σ=12. Samples of n=36 are drawn.

  1. State the mean of the sampling distribution.
  2. Calculate the standard error.
  3. A sample has x̄=54. Calculate its z score.
  4. If the upper-tail area beyond z=2.00 is .0228, find P(x̄≥54).
  5. Would x̄=54 be more or less unusual if n=9? Explain with a calculation.
TipAnswer
  1. μ_x̄=50.
  2. SE=12/sqrt(36)=2.
  3. z=(54-50)/2=2.00.
  4. P(x̄≥54)=.0228.
  5. Less unusual. For n=9, SE=12/3=4 and z=(54-50)/4=1.00.

Integrated Problem 2

Weeks covered: 2, 3

A dataset contains scores: 3, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 10.

  1. Construct the frequency values for each observed score.
  2. Calculate the mean.
  3. Find the median.
  4. Find the mode.
  5. Find the range.
  6. Describe the likely direction of skew and state which measure of center is more resistant to the high score.
TipAnswer
  1. Frequencies: 3:1, 5:2, 6:3, 7:1, 10:1.
  2. Mean = 48/8 = 6.
  3. Median = 6.
  4. Mode = 6.
  5. Range = 10-3 = 7.
  6. The high score creates a slight positive/rightward pull; the median is more resistant to that extreme value.

Integrated Problem 3

Weeks covered: 1, 5

A researcher draws repeated random samples from a population.

  1. Explain sampling error.
  2. Define a sampling distribution of the mean.
  3. State the three CLT properties emphasized in the course when n is sufficiently large.
  4. Explain why increasing n reduces standard error.
TipAnswer
  1. Sampling error is natural sample-to-sample variation.
  2. The sampling distribution is the distribution of all possible sample means for samples of a fixed size.
  3. Approximately normal shape; center at μ; SD of sample means equals σ/sqrt(n).
  4. n is in the denominator of SE=σ/sqrt(n), so larger n makes SE smaller.

Integrated Problem 4

Weeks covered: 1, 2

A researcher predicts that background television reduces concentration. Sixty students are randomly assigned to study either with television or in silence. Concentration is measured as number of targets correctly identified.

  1. State a reasonable research hypothesis.
  2. Identify the independent and dependent variables.
  3. Identify the research design.
  4. Is number of targets nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio?
  5. Which graph would be appropriate for displaying the distribution of target counts?
TipAnswer
  1. Example: students studying with background television will identify fewer targets than students studying in silence.
  2. IV: television condition; DV: number of targets correctly identified.
  3. Experimental design.
  4. Ratio scale (a count with a meaningful zero).
  5. A histogram is appropriate for the quantitative score distribution.

Integrated Problem 5

Weeks covered: 4, 5, 6

A population has μ=100 and σ=15. Consider samples of n=25 and an observed sample mean x̄=106.

  1. Calculate the raw-score z for an individual score X=106.
  2. Calculate the standard error for sample means of n=25.
  3. Calculate the sample-mean z for x̄=106.
  4. Explain why the two z scores differ.
  5. Which denominator belongs in the effect-size formula?
TipAnswer
  1. Raw-score z=(106-100)/15=.40.
  2. SE=15/5=3.
  3. Sample-mean z=(106-100)/3=2.00.
  4. Individual scores vary by σ, while sample means vary by SE, which is smaller when n>1.
  5. Effect size uses the population SD σ, not SE.
