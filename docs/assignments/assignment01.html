Question 1 — Summation Notation

Review

  • Week 01 §11 — Fundamental Mathematics
  • Week 01 §12 — Summation Notation
  • Week 01 §13 — Worked Summation Example
  • Week 02 §1 — Review: Summation Notation

Method

  1. Translate each expression into words before calculating.
  2. Identify exactly what is inside the summation sign.
  3. Follow BEDMAS/PEDMAS.
  4. Keep \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) paired by their common index.
  5. Distinguish \((\sum X)^2\) from \(\sum X^2\): one sums first and then squares; the other squares each observation first and then sums.
  6. For \(\sum(x_i-y_i)\) or \(\sum(x_iy_i)\), work row by row before adding.
  7. For \((\sum x_i)(\sum y_i)\), calculate the two sums separately and multiply only at the end.
  8. A constant inside a summation is applied once for every included observation.

Question 2 — Adding a Constant to Every Score

Review

  • Week 03 §15 — How the Mean Changes
  • Week 03 §33 — What Happens When Scores Are Transformed?

Represent the transformation as \(Y_i=X_i+c\). Consider the mean and standard deviation separately. For the mean, ask what adding the same constant to every observation does to the average. For the standard deviation, compare \(X_i-M_X\) with \((X_i+c)-(M_X+c)\) and use that comparison to explain what happens to spread.

Question 3 — Research Design and Causation

  • Week 01 §9 — Three Basic Research Designs
  • Week 01 §21 — Tutorial Preparation

Compare experimental, quasi-experimental, and correlational designs by asking whether there is manipulation, random assignment, and a comparison/control condition. Identify what distinguishes a true experiment, then explain how those features reduce competing explanations. Do not stop at saying that experiments allow causal conclusions; explain why.

Question 7 — Distribution Shape

  • Week 02 §12 — Ungrouped Frequency Distributions
  • Week 02 §23 — Frequency Polygons
  • Week 03 §18 — Distribution Shape and Skewness
  • Week 03 §20 — Other Distribution Shapes

Identify where most observations are concentrated and where the few extreme observations occur. The direction of the long tail determines the direction of skew. Your sketch should show the variable on the horizontal axis and frequency on the vertical axis.

Question 8 — Movie Ratings

8a — Measurement scales

Review Week 02 §4. Classify film rating and year separately by asking what information each variable preserves.

8b — Total frequencies

Review Week 02 §12 and Week 02 §13. Sum each rating column across all years, then add the rating totals to obtain the overall \(n\). Check the result by also summing the yearly totals.

8c — Percentages

Review Week 02 §13. For each category, calculate \(p=f/n\) and then percentage = \(p\times100\). The percentages should total approximately 100%.

8d — Visualization

Review Week 02 graphing sections and Week 02 §35 — Practice Questions. Identify whether film rating is categorical or continuous, choose the appropriate graph, and label both axes.

8e — Describe the graph

Review Week 02 §25 — Graphs Can Clarify—and Mislead. Describe the relative heights of the categories. Because this is categorical data, do not automatically impose continuous-distribution language such as positive or negative skew.

8f — Most popular rating

Review Week 02 §29 — The Mode and Week 03 §2 — The Mode. Compare the totals from 8b; the largest frequency identifies the modal category.

8g — Evaluate the claim

Use the frequency and percentage ideas from Week 02 §13 together with the mode. Translate the citizen's statement into something the table can test and base the conclusion only on the supplied fictional data.

Question 9 — Distracted vs. Not-Distracted Groups

Keep the two groups separate throughout the calculations.

9a — Mean

Review Week 02 §27 — The Mean and Week 03 §1. For each group, calculate \(M=\sum X/n\). Show the sum and \(n\), then divide.

9b — Range

Review Week 03 §22 — The Range. For each group, calculate \(X_{max}-X_{min}\).

9c — Standard deviation

Review Week 03 §24 — From Deviation to Variance, §39 — Important Terms, and §42 — Practice Questions. For each group: calculate the mean, deviations, squared deviations, and SS; apply the course's sample or population variance formula; then take the square root. Check the distinction between \(N\) and \(n-1\).

9d — Median and mode

Review Week 03 §2 — The Mode, §3 — The Median, and §4 — Median With an Even Number of Scores. Sort each group first. Locate the middle position for the median, and count frequencies for the mode.

9e — 75th percentile

Review Week 03 §5 — The Course's Interpolated Median Formula and §23 — Interquartile Range. Use the course's percentile procedure rather than substituting a different software convention. The 75th percentile is \(Q_3\).

9f — Compare the groups

Review Week 03 §36 — What Should Be Reported Together? and §38 — How the Concepts Fit Together. Compare mean, median, mode where useful, range, standard deviation, 75th percentile, and obvious distributional differences. Decide whether the descriptive summaries are consistent with the stated prediction. Do not call a difference statistically significant unless an inferential test has actually been conducted.

Final Self-Check

  • Show enough work for calculations to be followed.
  • Keep paired \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) values aligned in Question 1.
  • Distinguish \((\sum X)^2\) from \(\sum X^2\).
  • Justify measurement scales from their properties.
  • Label graphs clearly.
  • Use the course's percentile convention.
  • Distinguish sample from population variance where required.
  • Interpret Question 9 using both center and spread.
